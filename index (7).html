AeroDrop — Autonomous Delivery UAV

A quadcopter that takes off, flies a set of GPS waypoints, drops a payload on the target, and returns to land on its own, with no stick input after arming. I designed and 3D-printed the airframe with topology-optimized arms, checked the arm in SolidWorks FEA, and had the aluminum release CNC-machined. It runs ArduPilot on an STM32 board, planned in Mission Planner, for under $200 in parts.

02 About

I'm a mechanical engineering student in the Rutgers Honors College with a minor in computer science. Most of my time goes into drones: I design the structure in CAD, print and build it, then get the electronics and flight software working and take it out to fly.

I work across the whole build. Mechanical design and FEA in SolidWorks, plus parametric modeling in CadQuery. Embedded and flight work on STM32, Pixhawk, and Jetson boards, all wired and tuned by hand under ArduPilot and MAVLink. Scripting and analysis in Python and MATLAB. Fabrication with 3D printing, carbon-fiber tube, and light machining.

Last summer I interned at Novartis in radioligand-therapy manufacturing, keeping 34 pieces of production equipment qualified and inspection-ready under GxP. That was the regulated, reliability side of the same instinct I bring to my own builds: make it work, then make it hold up.
