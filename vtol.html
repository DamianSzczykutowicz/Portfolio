A fixed-wing aircraft that takes off and lands vertically like a quadcopter, then transitions to efficient forward flight on a pusher prop. Fully 3D-printed airframe, built on the Flightory Stork V.2 platform and tuned in ArduPilot.

// 01 — Overview

The idea

A VTOL quadplane gets the best of both airframes. Four lift rotors let it take off and land straight up, with no runway, so it can launch from anywhere. Once it is airborne, a pusher prop drives it forward and the wing carries the weight, which is far more efficient than hovering, so it covers real distance and endurance.

I built the whole airframe from 3D-printed parts on the Flightory Stork V.2 platform, then handled the propulsion, wiring, and ArduPilot setup, and flew it through the transition.

// 02 — Airframe & 3D Printing

Printed from the ground up

The skins are printed in PLA Aero, a lightweight foaming filament, on a Bambu Lab X2D. The motor pods are PETG, bonded to carbon-fiber booms with JB Weld so the lift motors sit out on stiff, straight arms. I also redesigned the nose section from scratch in CAD (CadQuery), including a faired variant sized for a Walksnail HD camera.

  • Skins: PLA Aero, tuned print settings for thin lightweight walls
  • Structure: PETG motor pods bonded to carbon-fiber booms
  • Nose: parametric redesign in CadQuery, multiple faired variants
// 03 — Propulsion & Power

Two flight modes, one power system

The vertical phase runs on four T-Motor F90 lift motors spinning 7-inch props on DShot600. Forward flight runs on a single SunnySky pusher on a PWM channel, split off from the lift group because of the flight controller's timer constraints. A Velox V50A 4-in-1 ESC drives the lift stack off a 4S 10000mAh pack.

Open item: at the ~3.2 kg target weight the lift ESC runs hot, above its rated all-up weight during the hover phase. I'm working the thermal side now: airflow to the ESC and current headroom, since hover is the most demanding part of the flight.

// 04 — Flight & Tuning

Making the transition work

It flies as an ArduPilot QuadPlane. Bring-up meant sorting the DShot and PWM split across timer groups, correcting the ESC output bitmask, and raising Q_ASSIST_SPEED to ~13–14 m/s so the wing takes over at the right point for the Stork's wing loading, well above the guide's default. Airspeed comes from an MS4525 sensor, and video is a Walksnail Avatar HD link.

The payoff is the clip below: a stable vertical hover, then the handoff to wing-borne flight.

Vertical hover during flight testing.

  • Controls: ArduPilot QuadPlane, DShot600 lift + PWM pusher
  • Tuning: Q_ASSIST_SPEED raised to ~13–14 m/s for the wing loading
  • Sensing: MS4525 airspeed, GPS, full telemetry
  • Result: validated the hover and the hover-to-cruise transition in flight
